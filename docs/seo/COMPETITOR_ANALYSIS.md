# Competitor analysis

Method: the Collaborator homepage was fetched and read in full (VERIFIED). For the other businesses the tools could surface only search-result excerpts of their own pages (VERIFIED, partial) and third-party roundups (THIRD-PARTY). A full crawl of titles, headings and internal links was **not** possible for them, so no claim below goes beyond what was seen. Re-run the "what to measure next" list with a crawler (Screaming Frog, Sitebulb, Ahrefs Site Audit) for complete data.

## 1. Collaborator (collaborator.pro): VERIFIED homepage
* **Title and meta description:** "Сollaborator — Digital PR and Content Distribution Marketplace" and "Marketplace for Digital PR and Content Distribution on top media outlets, news websites, blogs, and Telegram channels...". `robots: index, follow`, a canonical tag, a single H1.
* **Positioning:** "PR Distribution Marketplace", not "guest post marketplace". Claims 40K+ websites and 3K+ Telegram channels in the hero, 45,157 websites and 3,296 channels in the visible catalogue count, "158 countries and 53 languages".
* **Proof elements used:** award badges, an official Ahrefs-metrics integration, Google Analytics / Search Console verified-data counts, a free 3-month guarantee against deletion and non-indexing (with a paid 1-year extension), "over 75% of deals finalised within 48 hours", written customer quotes with names and companies.
* **Architecture:** audience-split navigation (advertisers / publishers / agencies), a public catalogue (`/catalog`), role pages (`/advertiser`, `/seller`, `/agency`), a blog (link insertions, how-to-get-backlinks-with-Collaborator), free tools (bulk URL checker, free backlink checker, SEO checklist), FAQ, many language versions.
* **What it does not do on the homepage:** it does not explain verification or what happens when a link disappears beyond the guarantee badge.

## 2. Adsy (adsy.com): VERIFIED excerpts, THIRD-PARTY claims
* Own pages seen: `/` ("Digital PR & Blog & Guest Posting Service"), `/guest-blogging-article-links` ("Buy Guest Posts With Relevant DA30+ Backlinks"), `/marketers` ("Place Blog Posts on 150K+ sites"), and many **niche landing pages** (`/sales-blogs`, `/seo-guest-posting`, `/shopping-blog`, `/news-guest-posting`) each titled "...blogs that accept guest posts".
* Claims on its own pages: 150K+ sites, filters by Ahrefs/GA4 traffic, DR, DA and spam score, "100% Money Back Guarantee", link-presence monitoring and an automatic content check after publication.
* **Takeaway:** Adsy wins long-tail "X blogs that accept guest posts" with many near-templated niche pages. That pattern is crowded and its thinness is a risk; PostAtlas should not copy it.

## 3. WhitePress: THIRD-PARTY plus one own page
* Roundups describe it as a content-marketing and link-building platform with 100,000-123,000+ websites in 30+ countries (claims). Its own knowledge-base page confirms a "Publisher Links" module (links in previously published articles, category/home pages, sitewide links). Capterra shows a 4.0/5 rating with comments praising publisher statistics and criticising limited publisher contact.

## 4. Links.me: THIRD-PARTY
* Roundups describe analytics per domain (traffic, region, sources, Ahrefs and Semrush data), collaboration tracking, and an "our-services" section with niche pages. No own-page data was retrievable.

## 5. PRPosting: NOT DIRECTLY ANALYSED
* Appears only in roundup lists ("data-driven metrics and niche segmentation"). No own page was retrievable here.

## 6. Others seen in results (names only, claims are THIRD-PARTY)
Posticy (marketplace with category pages), Loganix (stated 30,000+ sites, since 2011), Stan Ventures (manual outreach), GuestPostLinks, Vefogix, GuestPostSale, Backlinks.com, The Hoth, Rhino Rank, Authority.Builders, Linkhouse, Outreach Empire, Backlink Bazaar (mobile app). Backlink-management tools (Linkody, Pitchbox, BuzzStream) compete for the monitoring angle.

## SERP structure (ESTIMATE from what search returned)
For marketplace queries the results are dominated by (a) the big marketplaces' own pages and (b) "best / top N" roundups written by affiliates and agencies. A new brand will not win the head terms soon; it should win narrower, intent-matched pages and the "verification and monitoring" angle.

## Where PostAtlas can genuinely differ (all already true in the product)
| Opportunity | Evidence in competitors | PostAtlas fact it can state |
|---|---|---|
| Show how links are **verified and monitored** | Collaborator sells a deletion guarantee; Adsy says it monitors link presence; neither explains the method on the pages seen | Automatic page fetch checks link, target and attributes; re-check every 24 hours; one failed fetch never raises an issue; replacement goes to the publisher and the team |
| **Hour-based delivery deadlines with a live timer**, and a refund-or-alternative choice when missed | Collaborator advertises "75% of deals in 48 hours" as an average claim | A per-order deadline in hours, shown as a countdown, with a customer choice on a miss |
| **Honest data labelling** | Large catalogues lead with DR/traffic numbers | Reported vs verified figures are labelled; missing data is shown as missing |
| **Verified-buyer reviews per website** (not per publisher) | Collaborator shows per-site reviews | Reviews only after a verified placement; one per order; reviewer not named |
| **Free website check, no account** | Collaborator gates its catalogue behind sign-up | `/check` and publisher suggestions are open |
| Transparent, manual money handling | Card/PayPal/USDT/wire at Collaborator | Manual transfers confirmed by a person; PKR option with a locked rate (relevant to the Pakistani market) |

## What to measure next (needs tools)
Their title/H1/word-count per landing page, internal link counts, indexed page counts, referring domains, top organic keywords and traffic estimates (Ahrefs/Semrush), and Core Web Vitals field data (CrUX).
